# Interval-arithmetic implementation assurance audit

Status: **formal outward enclosure NOT ESTABLISHED**. This is a source-code audit of the predictive implementation, not a change to the physical model, algorithms, frozen results, or Proposition 2. The proposition remains a conditional mathematical statement: its positive-sign implication requires both sound state-tube propagation and a sound lower evaluation of the instantaneous reserve on every reachable state. The current executable monitor has not been shown to meet those premises. Its numerical outputs and the sampled checks are diagnostic evidence only.

## Executed path and numerical representation

The paper controller inherits `_predict` from `src/controllers/integrated_safety_controller.py`; `src/controllers/paper_safety_controller.py` replaces its uncertainty inputs but not its reserve callback. `_predict` calls `compute_predictive_reserve` in `src/safety/predictive_reserve.py`, which calls `propagate_interval_one_step` in `src/safety/reachable_set.py`. The common `Interval` class is in `src/safety_core.py`. Endpoints are ordinary Python `float` (binary64 on the evaluated CPython platform), with no configured IEEE-754 directed rounding, `nextafter`/`nexttoward`, interval library, or explicit endpoint inflation. Scalar input is converted with `float(value)`; decimal source parameters are already rounded when represented. Thus neither exact-real inclusion nor outward enclosure is guaranteed by the interval type.

## Elementary operations and dependencies

`Interval` addition/subtraction uses ordinary endpoint arithmetic and negation. Multiplication evaluates the four endpoint products and takes their floating-point minimum and maximum. `square` uses endpoint squares and returns zero as the lower endpoint when the argument spans zero. `reciprocal` evaluates the two reciprocal endpoints; division multiplies by that reciprocal. None of these operations rounds outward. `interval_clip` evaluates scalar saturation at the two endpoints with `min`/`max`, valid for its monotone clipping map in exact arithmetic, but still without a floating-point enclosure guarantee. `_nonnegative` applies endpointwise `max(0, x)`. The reachable map evaluates affine and polynomial expressions by ordinary natural interval extension; repeated variables are not symbolically simplified, affine-arithmetic tracked, or dependency-reduced. `min` over reserve components is performed on their computed lower scalar values after floating-point division by positive scales. There is no general interval `exp` operation on this executed predictive path, no general interval intersection or union API, and no discrete-branch hull construction. The low-speed zero-order-hold exponential appears in the pointwise instantaneous-row calculation, not as a proven interval extension of that branch.

## Branch and actuator handling

The backup extension in `src/safety/reachable_set.py` is an affine speed rule followed by componentwise saturation against bounds supplied by the current controller context. It encloses that particular affine-saturation formula in exact arithmetic if its inputs and bounds are valid, but is not a general enclosure of all mode-dependent backup decisions. `propagate_interval_one_step` carries `gear_id` unchanged and increments dwell time; it does not enumerate admissible gear changes or hull their outcomes. An interval for auxiliary availability multiplies the requested auxiliary command. Message age is advanced by sample time plus the communication-delay interval, while predecessor acceleration and jerk are interval inputs. This is not a proof of all packet-loss/rejection/stale-replacement branches. The instantaneous hard-row builder chooses the low-speed or high-speed thermal row at one point state; the predictor does not hull both certificate branches when a speed interval crosses the low-speed threshold. Likewise, the friction/fade and auxiliary limiting branches are not re-evaluated as interval functions on each tube state. No sound general `min`/`max` branch enclosure for those certificate rows is demonstrated.

Most importantly, the executed `_predict.lower` callback constructs reserve intervals by subtracting heuristic multiples of tube widths from the **current** instantaneous certificate (`0.02 * gap_width`, `1e-5 * brake-force widths`, and temperature width). No global Lipschitz or interval-evaluation argument establishes that those offsets bound the future collision, thermal, fade, auxiliary, or low-speed certificate over the tube. `compute_interval_certificate_lower_bound` then takes these supplied lower endpoints, but cannot make an unproven input interval sound. This is a theorem-to-code gap independent of rounding.

## Exceptional values and domain checks

`Interval.__post_init__` rejects a finite reversed interval (`low > high`) but does not explicitly reject NaN or infinity; comparisons with NaN can allow invalid endpoints through. Arithmetic overflow may yield infinity and underflow may round a nonzero endpoint to zero; there is no outward-recovery policy. `reciprocal` raises `ZeroDivisionError` for any denominator interval containing zero, including `[0,0]`. A zero-width interval at a nonzero denominator is accepted, subject to ordinary floating rounding. Validation requires positive lower endpoints for selected multiplicative uncertainties and nonnegative lower communication delay, but does not globally require finite interval endpoints. No empty-interval object or union of disjoint intervals is represented. A crossed-zero denominator is rejected rather than split into unbounded pieces.

## Evidence and decision

The predictive regression's 5,000 sampled-state containment comparisons check sampled tube behavior with a numerical tolerance; they are not exhaustive, do not prove roundoff enclosure, and do not validate the controller's heuristic reserve callback for every point in a tube. The 32,849 contemporaneous positive-sign checks likewise establish only observed consistency on stored samples. Theoretical `rho_i^{H,cert} >= 0` remains a valid one-sided implication **only if** the sound-enclosure assumptions of Proposition 2 hold. A negative predictive value remains an inconclusive warning, never a proof of future infeasibility. The executed values should not be presented as formally certified predictive lower bounds until directed/outward arithmetic, all relevant branch hulls, and a globally sound reserve extension are implemented and verified.

Decision: **Branch B**. `FORMAL IA IMPLEMENTATION GUARANTEE = NOT ESTABLISHED`; `REMAINING SCIENTIFIC ISSUE = IA IMPLEMENTATION ASSURANCE`. This document records the gap; it does not claim that an implementation repair or new validation experiment has been performed.
